We covered Modern Portfolio Theory and built it out in Python. The core idea: a portfolio's risk isn't just the average of its parts, because how assets move relative to each other matters just as much as how risky each one is on its own. Using simulated data across a few asset classes, we set up expected returns, volatilities and a correlation matrix, then ran a Monte Carlo simulation of thousands of random portfolios. Plotting them gave us the Markowitz bullet, and coloring each portfolio by its Sharpe ratio showed the efficient frontier and the portfolio with the best risk-adjusted return. It was a way to see why diversification works instead of just hearing it. We also introduced this semester's research projects and opened project applications.
