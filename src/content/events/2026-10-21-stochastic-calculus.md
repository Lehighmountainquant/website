We jumped into stochastic calculus, the math behind modeling things that move randomly over time. We started with Brownian motion, which traces back to botanist Robert Brown watching pollen particles jitter in water under a microscope. The pollen was being knocked around by countless tiny water molecules, and that same idea of a path that moves unpredictably but still follows clear statistical rules is how we model stock prices today. From there we built up to stochastic differential equations, which break a price's movement into a predictable drift plus a random shock. We also covered how an SDE is basically a regular motion equation from physics class with a noise term added on, and how the Black-Scholes equation for pricing options can be transformed into the heat equation.
